# Information-dense medical resume protocol

Use this protocol after the user confirms the fact card. Its purpose is to turn a small number of real experiences into a complete professional narrative without manufacturing evidence.

## 1. Plan before writing

For each experience, mark which dimensions are supported:

1. context or clinical/research background;
2. problem, objective or research question;
3. personal responsibility and boundary;
4. workflow or execution steps;
5. research method or study design;
6. software, databases, instruments or experimental techniques;
7. quality control, compliance or reproducibility;
8. decisions, interpretation or problem solving;
9. team collaboration and communication;
10. deliverables, outputs or confirmed results;
11. role-specific professional value.

Omit unsupported dimensions. Never use a generic template sentence to simulate a missing fact.

## 2. Allocate density by evidence

- Flagship, well-supported experience: normally 5–9 distinct bullets.
- Supporting experience: normally 3–6 distinct bullets.
- Sparse or observation-only experience: use fewer bullets and ask questions instead of padding.

Two bullets are duplicates when they use the same action, evidence and capability even if the wording differs. Keep only the stronger one.

## 3. Write three tiers from one fact set

All tiers must retain identical numbers, methods, tools, outcomes and responsibility boundaries.

- Conservative: foreground contribution and supervision.
- Professional: use confident action language, explicit methods and professional purpose.
- High impact: improve positioning, ordering and strategic relevance; never convert participation into ownership.

Professional is the default delivery version. Preserve the other two so the user can switch or select individual bullets.

## 4. Build a complete resume narrative

Use this default order for medical students and early-career candidates:

1. name, contact and target direction;
2. one-line candidate positioning and concise profile;
3. education;
4. research experience;
5. projects or leadership experience;
6. clinical experience;
7. publications, presentations or outputs when confirmed;
8. methods, tools, experimental techniques and compliance knowledge, grouped separately;
9. languages, certificates and supported research interests.

Avoid detached self-praise. Demonstrate capability through experience evidence, then use the profile to summarize the pattern.

## 5. Validate before delivery

Check that:

- every visible statement maps to a confirmed fact;
- every bullet adds a distinct dimension;
- no internal enum, evidence ID or placeholder is visible;
- no number, publication, clinical impact or responsibility is inferred;
- English technical terms are attached cleanly to Chinese text;
- the HTML contains no hard-coded candidate information;
- all template placeholders are replaced;
- the professional version is visibly information-dense without becoming repetitive.
